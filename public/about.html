crash-dash

How it works

Once per trading day, crash-dash pulls a set of free public market and economic series, scores each one against fixed thresholds, and rolls the lot into a single danger reading. That reading is the alert band at the top of the dashboard.

It watches, and when the band moves it sends a notification. That is the whole job. It places no orders, holds no account, and knows nothing about you.

Reading the alert band

Four steps, calm to acute.

OK
Nothing in the scored set is flagging. Ordinary conditions.
WATCH
Some signals have turned. Danger is building, but it is not broad yet.
WARNING
Several signals agree, and they sit in different parts of the market.
CRITICAL
Broad stress, everywhere at once.

The band moves up on the same run it is earned. It moves back down only after the calmer reading has held for two runs. That asymmetry is on purpose, because being late to escalate costs more than being early to relax. A change is pushed once. An unchanged band is not re-sent.

What it watches

Credit conditions. Interest rates and the shape of the yield curve. Equity volatility and its term structure, price trend, market breadth, valuation, plus a small geopolitical channel that is mostly quiet.

Every series gets its own card below the band, with a Green/Amber/Red state and its history, so any reading can be traced back to the signals behind it. No single indicator can carry the band on its own.

The panels below the band

Three more panels sit under the headline. None of them can move the alert band, which is deliberate.

  • Portfolio drawdown - how far a reference book of broad market exposure has fallen from its own peak, in EUR. This is decline that already happened, not risk ahead.
  • Bottom signal - a contrarian read of extreme, cresting volatility. It hints that fear may be burning itself out. It is not a buy signal.
  • Bubble state - a slow gauge of how much speculative fuel has piled up. Higher means more froth. It says nothing about when.

Where the numbers come from

Free public sources only: FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis), the European Central Bank, and daily closing prices from public market feeds. The full list, with the terms each source is used under, is on the Attribution page. What you see here are values crash-dash derived from those feeds, not figures the sources published.

Data arrives late, gets revised, and is sometimes simply wrong. Every card shows the age of its newest observation, so a stale feed is visible instead of quietly rotting behind a number.

What it deliberately does not do

  • No investment advice, and no personal recommendation, to anyone.
  • No account, no profile, no holdings. It knows nothing about you.
  • No orders, no broker connection.
  • No forecast, and no probability of a crash. It reports the present.
  • Nothing for sale, no advertising.
